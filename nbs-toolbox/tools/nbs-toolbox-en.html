Nature-based Solutions

Building a River-Basin Nature-based Solutions Planning Method and Practical Case Studies

Compiles international Nature-based Solutions (NbS) guidance, indicators and models, intervention measures, and case-study literature, and provides a planning flowchart to help teams ideate, design, and review Nature-based Solutions in river-basin governance.

“Nature-based Solutions are actions to protect, sustainably manage and restore natural and modified ecosystems in ways that address societal challenges effectively and adaptively, to provide both human well-being and biodiversity benefits.” IUCN Definition of Nature-based Solutions — Cohen-Shacham, E., Walters, G., Janzen, C., & Maginnis, S. (2016). Nature-based solutions to address global societal challenges. IUCN: Gland, Switzerland.

How to Use the NbS Introduction Process / Profile I, II, III (Original Template Notes)

NbS Introduction Process

Corresponds to the five-stage flowchart in the upper half of the "Solution Planning Flow" page. This item is used together with "NbS Profile II," echoing the IUCN NbS self-assessment standard: the five stages first clarify the project's overall rhythm — from initiation and understanding the system through to monitoring and evaluation — and then Profile II is filled in field by field with the planning content needed at each stage.

NbS Profile I

This profile briefly introduces how the applied NbS "protects, sustainably manages, restores nature, and transforms ecosystems to address societal challenges while providing human well-being and biodiversity benefits." It is presented as a simple "Intervention Measure → Societal Challenge" diagram, suitable for quickly explaining the overall logic of an NbS case at the start of a report or presentation — no detailed data entry is required.

NbS Profile II

Establishes a planning method for river-basin Nature-based Solutions. This template covers the key elements of an NbS, echoing the IUCN NbS self-assessment standard. It is designed from the planner's perspective, to support ideating and designing an NbS, taking stock of considerations, and presenting it to others. Because toolbox users — hydrology/hydraulics specialists — are familiar with hydrological and hydraulic models, several fields are drawn by analogy to simulation models (see the "Simulation-Model Perspective" hint on each field of that page). The "Planning Elements Diagram" further down that page is the interactive, fillable version of this profile, with each field labeled with its corresponding IUCN NbS global standard indicator number and toolbox category.

NbS Profile III

Used only to introduce NbS outcomes to the general public: it condenses the full planning content into four fields — "Case Background and Challenges Faced," "Actions and Goals," "Outcomes of the Actions: results, problems raised, or benefits," and "Key Lessons and Experience from the Case (e.g., factors behind its success or failure)" — suitable for public sharing, outcome reports, or external presentations; detailed planning should still be completed first using Profile II.
