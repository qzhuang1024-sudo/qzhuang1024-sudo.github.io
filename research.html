Research

How do old and new water meet, mix and separate in the pore space?

Motivation: ecohydrological separation

Stable-isotope field studies suggest that water held tightly in small pores ("old" water) can remain isotopically distinct from newly infiltrating water that moves through larger pathways. Whether — and how — this separation persists depends on pore-scale processes that are hard to observe in real soil. I use transparent micromodels to watch these processes directly.

Chapter 1

Pore-doublet micromodels: evaporation and pre-event water entrapment

Using pore-doublet micromodels, I examined how evaporation before a wetting event controls how much pre-event water is entrapped and how pre-event and event water interact when new liquid invades.

Huang et al. (2023), Advances in Water Resources.

Chapter 2

Grain-based pattern micromodels: separation during wetting cycles

Extending to more realistic grain-based pore geometries, I tracked the separation of old and new wetting liquids across repeated wetting cycles.

Huang et al. (2024), International Agrophysics.
